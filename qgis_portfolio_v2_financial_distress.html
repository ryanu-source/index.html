Methodology

  • 1

    Data acquisition

    Downloaded Census TIGER/Line county shapefiles, ACS 5-year estimates (income, poverty), BLS LAUS unemployment, and FFIEC 90+ day delinquency rates. Standardized 5-digit FIPS codes across all sources.

  • 2

    Spatial join via FIPS key

    Used QGIS "Join Attributes by Field Value" to merge tabular datasets onto the county polygon layer. Validated with Field Calculator and a PyQGIS script to flag nulls and mismatched FIPS codes.

  • 3

    Composite index construction

    Built a Financial Distress Index using weighted z-scores: delinquency rate (35%), unemployment (30%), poverty rate (20%), and inverted median income (15%). Computed entirely in the QGIS Field Calculator.

  • 4

    Choropleth & print layout

    Applied 5-class quantile graduated symbology with an amber-to-red sequential ramp. Built the print layout with scale bar, north arrow, legend, and source attribution for PDF export.

  • 5

    Hotspot analysis (Getis-Ord Gi*)

    Ran spatial autocorrelation to identify statistically significant distress clusters. Overlaid Federal Reserve district boundaries to contextualize regional banking exposure.

Key findings

Appalachian & Delta cluster

Eastern Kentucky, West Virginia, and Mississippi Delta counties landed in the top decile of the FDI, driven by 90+ day delinquency rates of 12–18% and unemployment well above the national mean.

Delinquency as strongest predictor

Consumer debt delinquency showed the highest spatial correlation with overall FDI score (Pearson r = 0.71), outperforming unemployment alone — suggesting debt servicing capacity leads county-level financial stress.

Federal Reserve District 4 (Cleveland) exposure

District 4 contained a disproportionate share of high-distress counties relative to its geographic footprint, indicating elevated community bank credit risk concentrated in that region.
